## Life

James Carson Marshall Shirk was a Philadelphia architect and one of the early Philadelphia architects to study at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris. He was born in Philadelphia, the son of U.S. Navy Commander James Whitehill Shirk and Mary W. Marshall.

Shirk graduated from Central High School in 1886 and apprenticed with Philadelphia architect Addison Hutton. In September 1888 he went to Europe for fourteen months and studied at the École des Beaux-Arts in the Atelier Pascal. After returning to Philadelphia in 1891, he briefly practiced as Hillman & Shirk with fellow Hutton alumnus Charles L. Hillman. In 1893 he established an independent practice in the Philadelphia National Bank Building.

His work was chiefly residential, but he also designed institutional buildings. His documented commissions include the Children's House of the Home for Incurables in Philadelphia and the Ward Garage & Stable in Bala Cynwyd. Other commissions included work for the Pennsylvania State Hospital for the Criminally Insane in Harrisburg and the Philadelphia Home for Incurables. He also reported winning first prize for the War Library and Museum for the Loyal Legion, first prize for a building in Erie—probably the Marine National Bank—and third prize for the Pennsylvania State Building for the World's Fair.

Shirk joined the T-Square Club in 1891 and the American Institute of Architects in 1901. The AIA historical directory lists him as an American architect and records his membership until his death. The Philadelphia Architects and Buildings database reports that he was residing in Scranton, Pennsylvania, when he died in 1918.

## Family context

His father, James Whitehill Shirk, was a U.S. Navy commander who served with distinction during the Civil War, including command of the gunboat USS *Lexington* at Shiloh and later the USS *Tuscumbia* during the Vicksburg campaign. The later destroyer USS *Shirk* was named for him.

James Carson Marshall Shirk's daughter Anne Bell Shirk later married Albert Cameron Burrage and became a founder of The Herb Society of America. The society was formally organized on August 23, 1933, at her home in Ipswich, Massachusetts.

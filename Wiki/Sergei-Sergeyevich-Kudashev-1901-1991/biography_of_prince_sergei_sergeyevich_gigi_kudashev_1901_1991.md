# Prince Sergei Sergeyevich Kudashev

**Prince Sergei Sergeyevich Kudashev** (known within his family as **"Gigi"**; French: *Prince Serge Koudacheff*; Russian: Князь Сергей Сергеевич Кудашев; 15/20 October 1901 – 14 April 1991) was a Russian-born aristocrat and émigré of the Kudashev princely family.\[1\]\[2\]\[3\] The only son of Imperial Russian courtier and art collector Prince Sergei Vladimirovich Kudashev and Countess Vera Maximilianovna von Nieroth, he escaped Petrograd with his parents and sister following the 1917 Russian Revolution and spent the remainder of his life in Western Europe, primarily in France.\[1\]\[4\]

## Early life in Russia and Italy

Sergei Sergeyevich Kudashev was born in Saint Petersburg on 15 October (or 20 October in some genealogical records) 1901.\[1\]\[2\] Through his father, Prince Sergei Vladimirovich Kudashev (1863–1933)—a *Kammerherr* (Chamberlain) at the court of Emperor Nicholas II and prominent corporate director—he belonged to the Kiev Governorate branch of the Kudashev princely dynasty.\[3\]\[5\] Through his mother, Countess Vera Maximilianovna von Nieroth (1874–1920), a former Maid of Honor (*freilina*) to Empress Alexandra Feodorovna, he was connected to the Baltic-German von Nieroth aristocracy and the Russian Trepov administrative family.\[1\]\[6\]

As a young child in St. Petersburg, Kudashev experienced episodes of fragile health. In the autumn of 1904, his parents brought three-year-old Sergei to Florence, Italy, for an extended stay aimed at aiding his recovery—a sojourn during which his father began corresponding with European art historians regarding the sale of his St. Petersburg Old Master painting collection.\[4\] Growing up in St. Petersburg alongside his older sister, Princess Maria "Myra" Sergeyevna Kudasheva (1895/1896–1990), he lived in the family's town residences on Mokhovaya Ulitsa and later at Bol'shaya Morskaya Ulitsa, 57, directly across the Moika Canal from the Yusupov Palace.\[4\]\[7\]

## 1917 Revolution and exile

Following the 1917 Russian Revolution and the Bolshevik seizure of power in October 1917, the Kudashev family's Imperial court standing and financial holdings placed them in immediate danger.\[3\]\[4\] With the assistance of young American diplomat Norman Armour—then Second Secretary at the United States Embassy in Petrograd, who procured false identity documents for the family—sixteen-year-old Sergei escaped across the Finnish border during the winter of 1917–1918 alongside his parents and sister.\[3\]\[8\]

From Finland, the family traveled to neutral Sweden, residing at Villa Skärtofta in Saltsjöbaden near Stockholm.\[8\] In February 1919, his sister Maria married Norman Armour in Stockholm (with a civil ceremony in Brussels), integrating the family into international diplomatic circles.\[8\] Following the death of his mother, Princess Vera, in Paris in January 1920, Kudashev spent his young adulthood between France—where his maternal grandmother Countess Anastasia Fyodorovna von Nieroth (née Trepova) and maternal uncle Major General Count Fyodor Maximilianovich von Nieroth had settled in exile—and Italy, where his father resided in Florence.\[1\]\[4\]

In September 1926, when King Victor Emmanuel III and Prime Minister Benito Mussolini issued a Royal Decree formally recognizing his father's princely title in the Kingdom of Italy and entering the family into the *Libro d'Oro della Nobiltà Italiana*, the patent specified that the title was transmissible within Italy exclusively to legitimate and natural male-line descendants, designating Sergei Sergeyevich as his father's dynastic heir.\[9\]

## Later life and death

Throughout his adult life in Western Europe, Kudashev remained closely connected to the Russian émigré community in France and to his sister Myra and brother-in-law Norman Armour as the latter served in senior United States diplomatic posts across Europe and the Americas.\[8\]\[10\]

Prince Sergei Sergeyevich Kudashev died on 14 April 1991 at the age of 89.\[1\] He was interred in the Nieroth–Kudashev family tomb in Division 93 (Avenue Circulaire, 1st line) of Père-Lachaise Cemetery in Paris, alongside his mother Princess Vera Maximilianovna Kudasheva (d. 1920), his maternal grandmother Countess Anastasia von Nieroth (d. 1940), and his maternal uncle Count Fyodor Maximilianovich von Nieroth (d. 1952).\[1\]

# Barbara Armour Lowrie

**Barbara Armour Lowrie** (27 November 1889 – 14 June 1981) was the only daughter of George Allison Armour of Chicago and Princeton, New Jersey, and the wife of the Episcopal clergyman and Kierkegaard translator [Walter Lowrie](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Walter-Lowrie-1868-1959/README-Walter-Lowrie-1868-1959.md). After his death she gave her family's house at 83 Stockton Street, Princeton, to Princeton University in his memory; as the Walter Lowrie House it has been the residence of the university's president since 1968.[1][2][3] She was the sister of [Norman Armour Sr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982/README-Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md) and Theo Armour's great-aunt.[1][8]

## Family and early life

Barbara Armour was born on 27 November 1889, in Chicago according to the profile page and the MyHeritage tree.[1][6] She was the daughter of George Allison Armour and Harriette Cobb Foote, and a granddaughter of the Chicago grain merchant [George Armour](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/George-Armour-1812-1881/README-George-Armour-1812-1881.md), whose wife was Barbara Allison.[1][7][8] Two family trees give her full name as Barbara Allison Armour.[6]

In 1895 her father bought the house at 83 Stockton Street, Princeton, which had been built for John P. Stockton and later occupied by Paul Tulane. *A Princeton Companion* says it "became the childhood home of Barbara Armour and her four brothers".[2] One of the brothers was the diplomat Norman Armour Sr.[1] Find a Grave links her to three others: William (1892–1958), Allison (1896–1955) and Edmund (1899–1912).[5]

A family tree cites the 1900 census for her at Princeton, a student and a daughter of the household (not seen).[6]

## Marriage and Rome

On 19 February 1918 she married the Rev. Walter Lowrie at Trinity Church, Princeton.[4] He was then rector of St. Paul's American Church in Rome, and twenty-one years older than she was.[4] They had no children.[4] A family tree cites a passport application of 1919 that connects her with Italy (not seen).[6]

Her mother died at Princeton on 11 October 1924.[6]

## Princeton

Barbara and Walter Lowrie "took up residence" at 83 Stockton Street in 1930, "following their return from Rome".[2] Her father died at Princeton on 8 June 1936.[6][7] The Princeton & Slavery Project says she inherited the property from him.[9] Family trees cite records of her at Princeton in 1935 and in the 1940 census (not seen).[6] During these years her husband wrote his books on Kierkegaard and made his translations.[2][4]

Walter Lowrie died on 12 August 1959.[4] After his death she and his executor, Canon Howard A. Johnson, went on giving his papers to Princeton University Library.[4]

## The gift of the house

On 25 February 1960 she gave the house to Princeton University, in memory of her husband, to be used as a guest house.[3] *A Princeton Companion* records the gift:[2]

> Lowrie, Walter, House at 83 Stockton Street was given to the University in 1960 by Barbara Armour Lowrie in memory of her husband, Walter Lowrie 1890. It was used as a University guest house until 1968, when it became the residence of the president.

## Death

She died on 14 June 1981, at Princeton according to the profile page and two family trees.[1][6] She is buried in Princeton Cemetery, where her husband is also buried.[5] Family trees place the burial of both her parents at Princeton as well.[6] No obituary has been found.

## Residences

* **Chicago, Illinois.** Born there in 1889, according to the profile page.[1]
* **83 Stockton Street, Princeton, New Jersey**, from 1895, her father's house.[2]
* **Rome, Italy**, after her marriage in 1918 until 1930. The sources read say only that the couple returned from Rome in 1930; her own movements in those years are not recorded.[2][4]
* **83 Stockton Street, Princeton**, from 1930 until she gave the house to the university in 1960.[2][3]
* **Princeton**, where she died in 1981. Her address after 1960 is not known.[1]

## Open questions

* **Year of birth.** The profile page, Find a Grave, MyHeritage and two family trees give 27 November 1889.[1][5][6] The Geni export gives "about 27 November 1890".[6]
* **Place of birth.** The profile page, Geni and MyHeritage give Chicago.[1][6] Two other trees say only Illinois, and Find a Grave gives no place.[5][6] Her brother Norman was born at Brighton, England, in October 1887.[8] No birth record has been seen.
* **Year of death.** The profile page, Find a Grave and two family trees give 14 June 1981.[1][5][6] The Geni and MyHeritage exports give only "1982".[6] The folder name and Find a Grave agree on 1981.
* **Place of death.** Princeton, according to the profile page and two trees.[1][6] Find a Grave gives no place of death.[5]
* **How many brothers.** *A Princeton Companion* says four.[2] The profile page, from Geni, lists seven: George, Paul, Norman, William R., Allison Vincent, Edmond and Liam.[1] Find a Grave links four: Norman, William, Allison and Edmund.[5]
* **When the house was built.** *A Princeton Companion* says 1845.[2] The Princetoniana Museum's reference page gives 1848–1849, and prints "1830" for the year the Lowries moved in, which must be 1930.[3]
* **How the house came to her.** The Princeton & Slavery Project says she inherited it from her father.[9] No will or deed has been seen, and she had brothers living in 1936.[5]
* **Her mother's birthplace.** Geni gives Chicago; an Ancestry-derived tree, citing Find a Grave, gives Wiscasset, Maine.[6]

# Additional Notes Not in README — Norman Armour Sr

Extracted on 2026-11-21 from `Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md` by comparison against `README-Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md`.

## Personal Recollections & Comments

Verbatim from the `## My Comments` section of `Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md`:

> Vladimir Lenin’s return to Petrograd was on April 3–16, 1917. Grandfather and a friend of his walked by and heard the turmoil of Lenin giving his speech. They wondered about stopping to listen, but nonetheless decided to continue their destination where they would play a game of whist with friends.
>
> When they were closing the embassy in 1918, the Embassy were told to burn all the papers. While doing so, it was time to close the door on the safe where the papers were kept. They knew the Bolsheviks would be certain to try and open the safe to see what was inside. Having a sense of whimsy, they decided to leave a symbol of capitalism, so they placed a top hat in the safe.
>
> When finally leaving Petrograd, Grandfather always said that he left on the train sharing a cabin with three sailors and a prostitute while sitting on a trunk full of jewels.
>
> Grandfather always said that he attended the party at Prince Yusupov's Palace the night that Rasputin was murdered.
>
> My grandfather was always amused that at his wedding with Granny, the ceremony being Orthodox  they had to walk around the church three times.
>
> Grandfather was involved with the North Russia Intervention in 1918-1919. There's a picture somewhere of him in Vladivostok.
>
> My father Norman Armour Jr was born in Brussels on February 10 1920

## Alternate Names, Spellings & Locations

- **Style / Honorific**: `The Honorable Norman Armour, Sr`
- **Birthplace**: `Brighton, East Sussex, England, United Kingdom` (the README lists `Brighton, England` without the county `East Sussex`).
- **Death place**: `New York, New York, United States` (the README lists `New York, New York`).

## Discrepancies vs. README

- **Number of Chief of Mission posts**: `Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md` states that he "served as Chief of Mission in eight countries", though the following sentence lists seven countries (`Canada, Haiti, Chile, Argentina, Spain, Venezuela, and Guatemala`). The README lists seven countries as Chief of Mission.
- **Wedding date annotation**: Under `## Marriage of Maria Kudasheva and Norman Armour`, `Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md` notes `1919-02-03 ~ February 2nd, 1919` (reflecting *The Washington Herald* of 4 February 1919 reporting the marriage as "yesterday", alongside 2 February 1919). The README states 2 February 1919.
- ***Washington Herald* summary wording**: `Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md` summarizes the notice as `Mr. Armour, whose house is in Chicago, was former second secretary of the American embassy in Petrograd and left there last September.` The README quotes the newspaper clipping as `whose home is in Chicago`.

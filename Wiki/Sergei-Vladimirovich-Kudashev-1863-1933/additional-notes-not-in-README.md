## Additional Biographical & Family Details

### Imperial Court Service & Main Department of Apanages (from `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md`)

* Notes that Sergei's court positions were primarily intended for ceremonial duties, including state receptions and accompanying the imperial family on official occasions.
* Specifies that the Main Department (Directorate-General) of Apanages operated as an independent unit within the Ministry of the Imperial Court, with four core tasks:
  * **Property management:** supervising the 'apanage estates' (vast areas, forests, and agricultural lands specifically designated to generate income for the grand dukes, grand duchesses, and other members of the House of Romanov).
  * **Peasant administration:** until the emancipation reforms of the 1860s (under Tsar Alexander II), administering millions of 'apanage peasants' who lived and worked on these royal lands, collecting taxes and managing their agricultural production.
  * **Financial management:** funding the allowances, stipends, pensions, and dowries of the extended imperial family from department revenues.
  * **Development of estates and enterprises:** financing and managing the construction, maintenance, and economic development of royal residences, industrial enterprises (such as wineries and factories), and agricultural activities throughout the empire (such as the imperial estate in Livadia in the Crimea).
* Gives the exact dates of the Winter Palace Romanov costume ball as **February 11 and 13, 1903**, noting that photographs of Princess Vera Kudasheva were also taken on that occasion.

### Extended Family & Diplomatic Cousins (from `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md`, `2026-11-14-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933-biography.md`, `2026-07-08-Erik-many-changes.md`, and `2026-07-16-erik-new-findings.md`)

* **Countess Vera Maximilianovna von Nieroth's parents:**
  * Father: **Count Maximilian Carl Benedict von Nieroth (1846–1914)**, an imperial chamberlain and master of the hunt of Baltic-German descent.
  * Mother: **Anastasia Fyodorovna Trepova (1849–1940)**, a former maid of honour in service of Tsarina Maria Feodorovna (1847–1928), wife of Tsar Alexander III (1845–1894).
* **Cousin Prince Nikolai Alexandrovich Kudashev** (dated `1868 1921` in `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md`; see Discrepancies):
  * First secretary at the Russian embassies in **Tokyo (1902)** and **Constantinople (1906)**.
  * Member of the Russian delegation at the **1905 Portsmouth peace conference** ending the Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905).
  * Russia's chargé d'affaires in **Washington (1910–1913)**.
  * Appointed envoy extraordinary and minister plenipotentiary to **China in 1916**, stepping down in **1920** after China severed ties with the former tsarist regime.
* **Cousin Prince Ivan Alexandrovich Kudashev (1859–1933):**
  * Envoy to **Denmark (1906–1910)**.
  * Envoy to **Belgium and Luxembourg (1910–1916)**.
  * Ambassador to **Spain** until the revolution in **1917**.
* **Son Prince Sergei Sergeyevich Kudashev (Serge Sergeiewitch Koudacheff, 1901–1991):**
  * Identified by the family nickname **'Gigi'** in Erik Van der Graaf's 16 July 2026 note (regarding the son's poor health during the 1904–1905 stay at Villa Granduchessa in Florence).
  * Erik Van der Graaf's 8 July 2026 Gallica (Bibliothèque Nationale de France) research uncovered a **1947 photograph**, **three Paris home addresses**, a reference to an **unlisted marriage to a French woman**, and **Paris business ventures** he partnered in or financed throughout **1920–1950**.
* **Second wife & daughter Olga Pelà:**
  * `2026-11-14-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933-biography.md` describes Olga Erikovna von Pistohlkors as "a fellow Baltic-German/Russian aristocrat" and identifies daughter Olga Sergeyevna Kudasheva's (Olga Pelà's) husband as **Italian historian and translator Achille Pelà**.
* **Chigirin Uyezd estates:**
  * `2026-11-14-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933-biography.md` mentions the nearby settlement of **Kudashevo** alongside Veselyi Kut in Chigirin Uyezd.

### Flight from Russia, Villa Skärtofta & Norman Armour Timeline (from `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md`)

* **Escape route & Sweden stay:** Presumably crossed the **frozen Lake Ladoga or the Gulf of Finland on foot** during the winter to bypass border controls (noting Finland declared independence from Russia in 1917), arriving in Sweden in the **spring of 1917** and staying at Villa Skärtofta **until the middle or end of 1918**, where Prince Kudashev regularly organized parties and gatherings for wealthy Russian noble refugees.
* **Frans Blom proposal (summer 1918):** At Villa Skärtofta in the summer of 1918, daughter Maria met Danish explorer and archaeologist **Frans Blom (1893–1963)**, who proposed marriage to her; she declined.
* **Norman Armour (1887–1982) 1916–1918 timeline:**
  * Later US ambassador to Canada, Haiti, Chile, Argentina, Spain, Venezuela, and Guatemala; stationed in St. Petersburg since **1916**.
  * Documented Russian political developments for the embassy and US government during **1917–1918** (through the February 1917 revolution, the provisional government under Georgy Lvov (1861–1925) and Alexander Kerensky (1881–1970), and the October 1917 Bolshevik takeover under Vladimir Lenin (1870–1924)).
  * Following the March 1918 Allied intervention, US embassy staff were ordered in **July 1918** to evacuate St. Petersburg to **Vologda** (over 500 km east).
  * After the August 30, 1918 assassination attempt on Lenin and the launch of the Red Terror, Armour was **arrested by the Bolsheviks in late August 1918**, taken to **Moscow**, and **detained for a month** over his role in the Kudashevs' 1917 escape.
  * Escaped and fled Russia using false papers via Finland to Sweden, arriving in **November 1918**, where he rejoined the Kudashev family and became **engaged to Maria Kudasheva that same month** before their February 1919 wedding.

## Discrepancies vs. README

* **Death date:** `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md` gives `Feb 28 1933` (README gives `27/28 February 1933`).
* **Children listed:** The Genealogy section of `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md` lists only two children (Maria Sergeyevna and Sergei Sergeyevich), omitting Olga Sergeyevna (b. 1926).
* **Daughter Maria's birth year:** `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md` gives `1895–1990` in the narrative (while linking to `Maria-Sergeyevna-Kudasheva-1896-1990`); README uses `1896–1990` and notes the discrepancy.
* **Cousin Nikolai Alexandrovich Kudashev's death year:** `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md` gives `1868 1921` (vs. README's `1868–1925`).
* **Date of escape from Russia & Villa Skärtofta stay:**
  * `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md` places the escape immediately after the **February 1917** revolution, arriving in Sweden in **spring 1917** and staying at Villa Skärtofta until the **middle or end of 1918** (README body places escape after the October 1917 revolution while noting the February 1917 alternative in its Discrepancies section).
  * `2026-11-14-...-biography.md` dates the Villa Skärtofta stay as **`c. 1918–1920`** (vs. README's `1917–1918`).
* **Art sale date range:** `0-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md` states the collection was "sold in 1902–1905 via two catalogues" (whereas 1902 was a private collection catalogue rather than a sale).
* **Painting titles & attributions in `koudacheff-collection.md`:** Lists Jan van Goyen works as *Cottages along a country lane* and *Village street with huts* rather than *A Sandy Road with Thatched Cottage* (README / profile page); also notes supplement discrepancies for Jan Brueghel entry 15 (*Bouquet of flowers in a vase*, labeled "de Jonge" with 1568–1625 dates) and Gerard ter Borch entry 8 (*The young abbot* vs. Christie's *Portrait of a young gentleman*).
* **Earlier biography draft (`2026-11-14-...-biography.md`) vs. revised README:**
  * Presents **Veselyi Kut** as the southern branch's "ancestral landed seat" without the README's correction that it passed to General Belsky's family and was sold in 1855.
  * States Sergei inherited an initial cabinet of paintings, rare books, and numismatics from his father in 1871 without the README's "Unsound" caveat.
  * Cites the original 1914 *Придворный календарь на 1915 год* (*Imperial Court Calendar for 1915*), St. Petersburg / Petrograd, 1914 directly, whereas the README cites N. Volkova's 2015 commentary edition.
  * Omits the 1926–1927 Italian Royal Decree / *Libro d'Oro* recognition, Erik Van der Graaf's / Ksenya Kiebuzinski's July 2026 findings, and the historiographical note on uncle S. S. Kudashev.

> **Business activities**
> Ksenya Kiebuzinski of the University of Toronto did an amazing job by screening and investigating Russian newspapers from the 1892-1917 period. This exercise revealed that your great-grandfather was involved in business on a large scale and far beyond my imagination. The newspapers revealed that he was involved in over 15 businesses - banks, insurance companies, railway companies, manufacturing companies and mining companies - mainly through membership of the board of these companies.
> I am currently working on a clear and detailed overview to be included in the next version of my report.
>
> **Private correspondence**
> With the help of the Berlin Art Museum I have had the opportunity to study various letters your great-grandfather sent to art historian Wilhelm von Bode who helped him to set up the catalogue of his painting collection in 1902.
> The correspondence covers the period between October 1904 and January 1905 and reveals the reasons why your great-grandfather wanted to sell off his collection. At that time he was residing in Florence and stayed there in view of the (bad) health of his son (that must have been 'Gigi'). His address in Florence back then was Villa Granduchessa.
> I need to study the correspondence more in detail and then I will include the key parts in the next version of my report.
>

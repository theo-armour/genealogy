# Harriette Cobb Foote Armour (1859–1924)

**Harriette Cobb Foote Armour** (15 November 1859 – 11 October 1924) was the wife of [George Allison Armour](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/George-Allison-Armour-1856-1936/README-George-Allison-Armour-1856-1936.md) and the mother of the diplomat [Norman Armour Sr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982/README-Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md). She was born in Maine, a granddaughter of [Erastus Foote](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Erastus-Foote-1777-1856/README-Erastus-Foote-1777-1856.md), the state's first attorney general. Her family moved to Chicago in 1868. She married there in 1882 and lived at Princeton, New Jersey, from 1895 until her death.[1][2] She was Theo Armour's great-grandmother, his father's father's mother.

## Life

### Family and early life

She was the youngest child of Erastus Foote (1821–1893), a lawyer of Wiscasset, Maine, and Sarah Page Wood. Her father moved the family to Chicago in 1868.[1][2] One family tree cites the 1860 census for her at Wiscasset, and the 1870 and 1880 censuses at Chicago, where in 1880 she was an unmarried daughter living at home.[4]

### Marriage and children

She married George Allison Armour, a Princeton graduate and son of the Chicago grain merchant [George Armour](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/George-Armour-1812-1881/README-George-Armour-1812-1881.md), in Chicago on 27 April 1882.[1] Her father and her husband's father had been ordained elders of the Second Presbyterian Church of Chicago on the same occasion, in April 1877.[1]

*Foote Family* lists seven children. George died of diphtheria in 1893 at the age of eight, and Paul died as an infant. Norman was born at Brighton, England, in 1887 while his parents were there.[1][2]

### Princeton

The family lived at Princeton, New Jersey, from July 1895. That year George Allison Armour bought the Italianate house at what is now 83 Stockton Street. Princeton University says it "became the childhood home of Barbara Armour and her four brothers". The house is now known as Walter Lowrie House and is the residence of the university's president.[1] One family tree cites the censuses of 1900, 1910 and 1920 for her at Princeton.[4]

In January 1920 her husband applied for a passport for both of them to travel to Italy, France and Belgium, where their son Norman was at the American Embassy in Brussels.[1] One tree records their return, sailing from Naples and arriving at New York on 27 May 1920.[4]

### Death

She died on 11 October 1924, at Princeton according to the Geni tree. She was buried at Princeton.[3][4]

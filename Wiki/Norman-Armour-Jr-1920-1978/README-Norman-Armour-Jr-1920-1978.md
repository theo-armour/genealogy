# Norman Armour Jr

**Norman Armour Jr** (10 February 1920 – 26 April 1978) was the only child of the American diplomat [Norman Armour Sr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982/README-Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md) and Princess Myra Koudacheff ([Maria Sergeyevna Kudasheva](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Maria-Sergeyevna-Kudasheva-1896-1990/README-Maria-Sergeyevna-Kudasheva-1896-1990.md)). He was born in Brussels, attended Princeton University, and in 1941 married [Cynthia Sewall Burrage](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Cynthia-Burrage-Hovey-1921-1983/README-Cynthia-Burrage-Hovey-1921-1983.md) of Boston and Ipswich, Massachusetts.[1][2][4] He was the father of Theo Armour.[1]

## Family and early life

Norman Armour Jr was born in Brussels on 10 February 1920.[1][6] His father, a secretary in the American diplomatic service, had been assigned to the Embassy in Brussels in December 1918 and had married in Stockholm on 2 February 1919.[7] His mother was the daughter of Prince [Sergei Vladimirovich Kudashev](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933/README-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md) and Countess [Vera Maximilianovna von Nieroth](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Vera-Maximilianovna-Kudasheva-von-Nieroth-1874-1920/README-Vera-Maximilianovna-Kudasheva-von-Nieroth-1874-1920.md); his grandmother Vera died in Paris on 26 January 1920, two weeks before he was born.[7][8] *Time* magazine called him his father's "only child".[4]

His father's assignments in his childhood were The Hague (1920), Montevideo (1921), Washington, Rome, Tokyo and Paris, followed by Haiti (1932–1935) and Canada (1935–1938) as Minister.[7][8][9] Family trees built from Ancestry records attach to him an entry as an infant in the 1920 census of Americans in the consular service abroad, an arrival at San Francisco on 2 June 1927, an entry in the 1930 census at Paris, and an arrival at New York from Port-au-Prince on 9 January 1933. None of these records has been seen for this biography.[6]

## Princeton and marriage

He attended Princeton University. One family tree cites the Princeton yearbooks of 1940 and 1941 for him (not seen).[6] In May 1941 *Time* described him as "21, Princeton junior".[4] In June 1941 the *Boston Sunday Post* described him as "of Princeton, N. J." and "a graduate of Princeton University" (see Open questions).[2]

*Time* reported his engagement on 5 May 1941:[4]

> Engaged. Norman Armour Jr., 21, Princeton junior, only child of U.S. Ambassador to the Argentine Norman ("The Ideal Diplomat") Armour; and Cynthia Sewell Burrage, 19, granddaughter of the late multimillionaire Boston copper king Albert Cameron Burrage; in Boston.

They were married on Saturday 28 June 1941 at Ascension Memorial Church in Ipswich, Massachusetts. The Rev. Richard A. Cartmell officiated, and H. Clay Frick of Roslyn, New York, was best man. The *Boston Sunday Post* of the next day reported the wedding under the headline "Cynthia Burrage and N. Armour, Jr., Wed", with a photograph of the couple.[2] The bride was the daughter of [Albert Cameron Burrage Jr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Albert-Cameron-Burrage-Jr-1886-1978/README-Albert-Cameron-Burrage-Jr-1886-1978.md) and [Anne Bell Shirk Burrage](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Anne-Bell-Burrage-1890-1983/README-Anne-Bell-Burrage-1890-1983.md).[2][10] A fuller account of the wedding is in her biography.[10]

## War years

Family trees cite a Second World War draft registration card for him in New Jersey and a record of him at Princeton in 1942, as a student (not seen).[6] His folder in this repository holds two photographs without captions. One shows a man in United States Army uniform, in a field jacket with an American flag patch on the sleeve and a garrison cap with an officer's bar, seated in an open vehicle; the other is a colour snapshot of a man swimming.[11] No record of his military service has been seen for this biography.

## Later life

The couple had three children.[3] The marriage ended in divorce in August 1952; the *Boston Post* reported the decree from Reno, Nevada, under the date 22 August 1952, and said that he was then living in Washington, D.C.[3]

He later married Cynthia Brooks Howe.[1][5][6] One family tree gives her dates as 27 July 1923 – 1 July 2004 and the marriage as 14 October 1952 at San Francisco, without sources.[6] The profile page lists four children in all.[1]

He died in New York on 26 April 1978, aged 58, and is buried in Locust Valley Cemetery, Locust Valley, Nassau County, New York.[1][5][6] His father outlived him by four years and his mother by twelve.[7][8] No obituary has been found.

## Residences

* **Brussels, Belgium.** Born there in 1920.[1]
* **With his parents at his father's posts**, 1920–1938: The Hague, Montevideo, Washington, Rome, Tokyo, Paris, Port-au-Prince and Ottawa. His own presence is recorded, in family trees only, at Paris in 1930.[6][7][8][9]
* **Princeton, New Jersey.** "Of Princeton, N. J." at his marriage in 1941.[2]
* **Washington, D.C.**, in August 1952.[3]
* **New York, New York**, where he died in 1978. Find a Grave gives Manhattan.[1][5]

## Open questions

* **Date of birth.** The profile page, Geni, MyHeritage and two family trees give 10 February 1920 at Brussels.[1][6] An Ancestry export gives 12 February 1921.[6] Find a Grave gives 10 February 1910 and an age at death of 68.[5] *Time*'s "21" in May 1941 supports 1920.[4]
* **Princeton: student or graduate in 1941?** *Time* in May 1941 calls him a "Princeton junior".[4] The *Boston Sunday Post* in June 1941, and the *Boston Post* in 1952, call him a graduate of Princeton University.[2][3] His class and degree have not been checked in Princeton's records.
* **Occupation.** The Geni and MyHeritage records give his occupation as "Diplomat".[6] No published record of a diplomatic appointment has been seen, and the description may have been carried over from his father. The 1952 newspaper report gives no occupation.[3]
* **Military service.** The photograph in his folder shows an Army officer's uniform, but it is not captioned and no service record has been seen.[11]
* **The second marriage.** The date and place (14 October 1952, San Francisco) come from one family tree without a source.[6] The profile page, following Geni, records his second wife's name as "Armour / Paine", which points to another marriage of hers; nothing about it has been verified.[1]
* **Number of children.** The 1952 report says three children of the first marriage.[3] The profile page lists four children.[1] The MyHeritage summary of his Geni record lists three.[6]
* **Place of burial.** Find a Grave and two family trees give Locust Valley Cemetery.[5][6] The Geni export gives Lattingtown, New York, the neighbouring village.[6]
* **A mixed record.** One FamilySearch-derived tree attaches his death and burial to the record of another member of the family, and attaches some of his father's records to him.[6] Events taken from that tree need checking against the original records.
* **Order of the marriages.** The profile page of his first wife lists her second husband before him. The newspapers show that her marriage to Norman Armour Jr came first.[2][3][10]

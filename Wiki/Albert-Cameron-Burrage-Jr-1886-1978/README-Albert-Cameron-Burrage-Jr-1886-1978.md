# Albert Cameron Burrage Jr

**Albert Cameron Burrage Jr** (30 August 1886 – 8 February 1978) was a Boston engineer and copper-mining executive, and a vegetable gardener and writer on gardening, who lived in Boston and at Ipswich, Massachusetts. He was a son of the attorney and copper industrialist [Albert Cameron Burrage Sr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Albert-Cameron-Burrage-Sr-1859-1931/README-Albert-Cameron-Burrage-Sr-1859-1931.md). The Massachusetts Horticultural Society gave him its Large Gold Medal in 1942 for research in vegetable selection, and he published *Burrage on Vegetables* in 1954.[1][2][3][4][5] He was the maternal grandfather of Theo Armour.[1][6]

## Family and early life

Albert Cameron Burrage Jr was born on 30 August 1886 in Boston, according to the profile page, which follows Geni's web page.[1] A family tree drawn from Ancestry also gives 30 August 1886, and cites Massachusetts birth records among its sources; other exports give 30 August 1887 (see Open questions).[7][8]

He was one of the four children of Albert Cameron Burrage Sr and Alice Hathaway Haskell, who brought them up at 314 Commonwealth Avenue in Boston. His brothers and sister were Francis Hathaway Burrage, Russell Burrage and Elizabeth Alice Burrage (Bessie Alice on the profile page).[1][2] One family tree cites the 1900 census for him as a student, living as a son in his father's household at Cohasset, Massachusetts.[7]

## Education and career

He graduated from Harvard in 1908 and became a metallurgist at Bayonne, New Jersey. He then returned to Boston as a mining engineer with companies that his father had developed, and lived with his parents at 314 Commonwealth Avenue.[2] *Back Bay Houses* describes him later as "an engineer and copper mining executive affiliated with companies developed by his father".[3] One family tree records his occupation as engineer at Hamilton, Massachusetts, in 1920 and 1921, and as manufacturer at the 1930 census.[7] Geni gives his occupation simply as "Businessman".[8]

## Marriage and children

He married **Anne Bell Shirk** (1890–1983), who was born in Philadelphia, in April 1915.[2][9] One family tree cites reports of the marriage in the *Boston Evening Transcript* of 7 April 1915 and the Knoxville *Journal and Tribune* of 11 April 1915.[7] They had three children:[1]

* **Albert Cameron Burrage III** (1919–1944), known as Cameron, a captain in the 6th Armored Division, killed in Brittany in August 1944.[10]
* **Anne Bell**, who by June 1941 was Mrs William B. Chace of Uxbridge.[1][11]
* **Cynthia Sewall** (1921–1983), who married Norman Armour Jr in 1941 and later Chandler Hovey Jr.[6]

At Cynthia's wedding at Ipswich on 28 June 1941, the newspapers described her parents as "Mr. and Mrs. Albert C. Burrage of Ipswich and 29 Hereford street, Boston". Her father gave her away, and the reception was held at "Candlewood Farm", described as the summer home of the bride's parents.[11]

In 1933 seven women met at his wife's home at Ipswich and formed The Herb Society of America.[9][12]

## Gardening and writing

Like his father, Burrage was a keen horticulturist, but his field was vegetables.[2][3] In 1942 the Massachusetts Horticultural Society awarded "Albert C. Burrage, Jr" of Boston its Large Gold Medal "for research in vegetable selection and other work".[4]

In 1943 Houghton Mifflin published *The Twenty-Minute-a-Day Garden*, copyright "Albert C. Burrage". The book reports on "the 1942 garden" and sets out plans for 1943. The publisher's note says that from a garden of twenty-five by fifty feet "Mr. Burrage supplies his family of four with vegetables from June through October".[13] The Internet Archive's catalogue entry attributes the book to Albert Cameron Burrage, 1859–1931, but the father had died twelve years before the garden it describes, so the author must be the son.[13][14]

In 1954 he published *Burrage on Vegetables* (New York: Van Nostrand).[2][3][5] *Kirkus Reviews* described the author as a layman who applied "his engineering approach" to the management of a home garden, and said that although the garden was in Massachusetts the book had much in it for any gardener.[5]

## Later life and death

The Burrages lived at 29 Hereford Street, Boston, until about 1946.[3] A history of an Ipswich house records that a property was left to Anne Bell Burrage by the will of Emily G. Patch in 1950, that in 1951 fifteen people met "at the Burrage home at 44 Beacon Street, Boston" and Anne Bell Shirk Burrage became the first president of the Herb Society's new corporation, and that in October 1953 Anne Bell Burrage, "wife of Albert Cameron Burrage Jr.", bought the lot at 74 Essex Road, Ipswich.[12] One family tree cites the 1950 census for him as head of a household at Ipswich.[7]

He died at Ipswich on 8 February 1978.[1][8] One family tree cites an item in *The Boston Globe* of 10 February 1978 and the Social Security Death Index for his death.[7] His wife died at Ipswich on 22 August 1983.[9] The same Ipswich house history shows a photograph of the "Burrage family tombstone at the Highland Cemetery in Ipswich", but does not say who is buried there.[12]

## Residences

* **Boston, Massachusetts.** 314 Commonwealth Avenue, his parents' house, as a child and again after his return from New Jersey.[2]
* **Cohasset, Massachusetts.** In his father's household at the 1900 census, according to one tree.[7]
* **Bayonne, New Jersey**, as a metallurgist after 1908.[2]
* **East Bridgewater, Plymouth County, Massachusetts.** Given as his residence in 1917–1918 by one tree, which cites his First World War draft registration.[7] *(Unsound: the draft card has not been seen.)*
* **Hamilton, Massachusetts.** His home after his marriage in 1915; at the 1920 census, according to one tree, and in 1921.[2][7]
* **29 Hereford Street, Boston**, from the winter of 1926–1927 until about 1946. His mother bought the house as trustee for his benefit.[3]
* **Ipswich, Massachusetts.** "Candlewood Farm", at first the family's year-round home and later its summer home; in the 1950 census, according to one tree; and his home at his death.[3][7][11]
* **44 Beacon Street, Boston.** Called "the Burrage home" in 1951.[12]

## Open questions

* **Year of birth.** The profile page (from Geni's web page) and one Ancestry-derived tree give 30 August 1886.[1][7] The Geni GEDCOM exports, MyHeritage's summary of Geni and one Ancestry export give 30 August 1887; another tree gives only 1887.[8] The profile page and the Geni export disagree with each other, although both come from Geni. His Harvard degree in 1908 fits 1886 better, but no birth record has been seen.
* **Where the family lived after 1915.** *Back Bay Houses* says, on one page, that they lived at Hamilton after their marriage, and, on another, that Candlewood Farm at Ipswich had been their year-round home before 29 Hereford Street.[2][3] The two may describe different periods; the sources do not give dates.
* **Authorship of *The Twenty-Minute-a-Day Garden*.** Catalogued under Albert Cameron Burrage Sr (1859–1931), but published in 1943 about a garden kept in 1942.[13][14] The local copy in this repository is filed under the father's name too.
* **The third child's name.** The profile page gives Anne Bell; MyHeritage's summary of Geni gives "Nancy Burrage".[1][8]
* **Mother's dates.** This biography does not repeat the mother's dates. The linked profile is named 1861–1947, while one Ancestry export gives her birth as 6 October 1857 at Hong Kong.[8]

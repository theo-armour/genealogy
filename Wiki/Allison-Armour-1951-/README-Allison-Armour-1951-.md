# Allison Armour

**Allison Armour** (born 1951), also known in earlier exhibitions and press as **Allison Armour-Wilson**, is an American-raised contemporary sculptor and garden designer known for her mirror-polished stainless steel sculptures and the *Aqualens* crystal sphere water fountain.[1][2][4][5] Born in Paris and raised in New York, she studied at the Parsons School of Design and the Architectural Association in London before launching her sculpture practice in Sussex, England, in 1999 and debuting with a Silver Medal at the 2000 RHS Chelsea Flower Show.[1][4][5] She is the daughter of [Norman Armour Jr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Norman-Armour-Jr-1920-1978/README-Norman-Armour-Jr-1920-1978.md) and [Cynthia Sewall Burrage](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Cynthia-Burrage-Hovey-1921-1983/README-Cynthia-Burrage-Hovey-1921-1983.md), and the sister of [Theo Armour](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Norman-Theo-Armour-III-1947-/Norman-Theo-Armour-III-1947-.md).[9][10][11]

## Family and early life

Allison Armour was born in Paris in 1951 and raised in New York.[1][11] She is a daughter of [Norman Armour Jr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Norman-Armour-Jr-1920-1978/README-Norman-Armour-Jr-1920-1978.md) and [Cynthia Sewall Burrage](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Cynthia-Burrage-Hovey-1921-1983/README-Cynthia-Burrage-Hovey-1921-1983.md).[9][10][11] Her paternal grandparents were the career diplomat [Norman Armour Sr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982/README-Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md) and Princess [Maria (Myra) Sergeyevna Kudasheva](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Maria-Sergeyevna-Kudasheva-1896-1990/README-Maria-Sergeyevna-Kudasheva-1896-1990.md); her maternal grandparents were [Albert Cameron Burrage Jr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Albert-Cameron-Burrage-Jr-1886-1978/README-Albert-Cameron-Burrage-Jr-1886-1978.md) and [Anne Bell Shirk Burrage](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Anne-Bell-Burrage-1890-1983/README-Anne-Bell-Burrage-1890-1983.md), co-founder of The Herb Society of America.[9][10]

She attended the Parsons School of Design in New York before moving to London to study at the Architectural Association.[1] In September 2000 *Homes & Gardens* reported that she had lived in England for 28 years, placing her move there around 1972.[4] On her website she credits her time at the Architectural Association with sparking her interest in gardens, outdoor sculpture, and water.[1]

## Origins of the *Aqualens* and the Chelsea Flower Show

Armour's career as a sculptor and garden designer began in 1999 in Sussex, England.[1][5] As she describes it on her website:

> "I was happily doing up my present house and couldn't find a water feature I liked for my own garden – so I decided to make one myself."[1]

According to a 2001 profile in *The English Garden*, a storm had blown down trees at her home, Baldhorns Park in Sussex, opening up space around the walled garden and lake; partly inspired by the clean lines and reflective surfaces of the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao, she set out to commission contemporary pieces of her own.[5] After encountering reluctance from fabricators, a local maker produced her first work, a single-piece clear perspex "garden chaise".[5] She soon created her signature water feature, initially called the *Aquasphere* and later the *Aqualens* — a clear, water-filled acrylic globe resting inside a circular marine-grade 316 stainless steel dish, over which water continuously wells up and cascades.[1][5][7] She later recalled finding the right-sized spheres "in an Italian car park, being used as lamps", and sending photographs of the finished piece to magazines at friends' urging.[1][5]

After showing her designs to plantsman Peter Rogers, she applied to the RHS Chelsea Flower Show and secured a plot for 2000 with nine months to prepare, backed by *Homes & Gardens* editor Matthew Line.[4] Her debut show garden, *Allison Armour-Wilson's Garden of Reflection*, juxtaposed the *Aqualens* and frost-hardy mirrored acrylic obelisks with white herbaceous borders, formal cedar hedging, and a pavilion designed by Glass Houses architect Jeffrey Gold.[4] It won a Chelsea Flower Show Silver Medal in 2000 and was voted best garden by Sky News.[1][4] She returned to Chelsea in 2001 with *Allison Armour-Wilson's Xceptional Garden*, winning a Bronze Medal.[1]

## Sculpture practice and artist's ethos

Following her Chelsea debut, Armour expanded into large-scale public, corporate, hospitality, and residential sculptures made primarily of marine-grade mirror-polished stainless steel and acrylic.[1][6][7] Alongside the *Aqualens* — produced in configurations ranging from 24-inch to 60-inch acrylic spheres set in stainless steel dishes up to 10 feet across — her portfolio includes *Moongate* (15 ft / 5 m diameter), *Steel Eye*, *Star* (17 ft / 5 m diameter), *Rosatom*, *Aspire*, *Pinwheel*, *Obelisk*, *Spiral*, *Concentric Circles*, *TeePee*, *Poles*, *Mobile*, *Mesa*, and *Double Cs*, as well as pergolas and clear acrylic rowing boats.[1][6][7]

On her website's Artist's Ethos page, she cites John Keats's *Ode on a Grecian Urn* (quoted there as *Ode to a Grecian Urn*: "Beauty is truth and truth is beauty, — that is all ye know, and all ye need to know") as her guiding principle, writing:

> "My art excites the environment of the space it occupies, reflecting the beauty of the world around it. I like to defy the reality we exist in – our day-to-day lives, the norm, the mundane – by creating anarchistic pieces that use geometry, steel, light, and water to create an escape for the viewer... My use of pure geometry is an expression and belief that perfection does exist, as does the absolute, however ephemeral it may be."[3]

## Community service

In Santa Barbara County, California, Armour has volunteered as a gardening teacher for young girls at Girls Inc. and served as an active board member of Sarah House, a nonprofit providing end-of-life care for low-income residents.[1]

## Media and publications

Her work has been featured in print publications including the *Financial Times*, *Daily Telegraph*, *Sunday Times Style*, *House & Garden*, *Homes & Gardens*, *Gardens Illustrated*, *The English Garden*, *Country Life*, *Elle Decoration*, *Architectural Digest* (Germany), *The Washington Post*, *Santa Barbara Magazine* (2016), *Montecito Journal Magazine*, and *Florida Design – Art & Design* (2024).[1][2] Broadcast appearances include BBC 2 (*Homefront in the Garden* and *Real Rakeovers*), Channel 4's Chelsea Flower Show coverage (2000 and 2001), ITV 1 (*The Richard and Judy Show* and Meridian live interview), Sky News, MTV, and Russian and Swedish television.[1][2] Her work is also included in *Garden Design* by John Brookes and *Das Garten Der Kunstlerin* by Charlotte Seeling.[1][2]

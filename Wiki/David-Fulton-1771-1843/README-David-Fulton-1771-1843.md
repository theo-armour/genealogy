# David Fulton

**David Fulton** (2 January 1771 – 7 August 1843) was an Irish-born settler who came to Maryland as a youth, moved west with his family through Tennessee and Alabama, and spent his last ten years in Little Rock, Arkansas. There he was the fourth and last mayor of the Town of Little Rock in 1835, a county judge, and surveyor-general of the public lands in Arkansas under President Van Buren.[2][3][5] He was the father of [William S. Fulton](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/William-S-Fulton-1795-1844/William-S-Fulton-1795-1844.md), governor of Arkansas Territory and United States senator, and through him Theo Armour's maternal great-great-great-great-grandfather.[7][10][12]

## Ireland and Maryland

David Fulton was born on 2 January 1771 in County Donegal, Ireland.[3][4][10] A local-history article reproduced on his Find a Grave memorial places the birth in the parish of Templemore and names his parents as David and Elinor Macken Fulton.[4]

The best evidence for his coming to America is his own. In a letter to his son William dated at Little Rock on 4 January 1841, printed in John Hallum's history of Arkansas (1887), he wrote:

> I have been one of the republican family since September, 1788, the memorable year our admirable constitution was adopted; when I happily, with father, mother and family became adopted members, my grandfather and his family having emigrated into Maryland three years previous.[2]

He was then seventeen. Hallum, who had the family's papers from David's granddaughter Elizabeth Wright, describes him as "a thoroughly educated and polished Irish gentleman of the old school" who "came to America in his youth and settled in Maryland".[2]

He married Elizabeth Savin, whom Hallum calls "of the eastern shore" of Maryland.[2] Their son William Savin Fulton was born in Cecil County, Maryland, on 2 June 1795.[2][6] The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says that William's schooling was owed to "his mother's wealth".[6] A parish history of 1900 calls David "Judge David Fulton, of Baltimore, Md."[7]

Hallum says that David "was one of the gallant defenders of Fort McHenry in the war of 1812", and the Encyclopedia of Arkansas says that William served in the Baltimore Fencibles, a volunteer unit, "alongside his father" before the bombardment.[2][6]

## Tennessee and Alabama

In 1815 David's eldest daughter, Mary, married William Crockett, a merchant of Gallatin, Tennessee. According to Hallum the marriage "caused the removal of the family to Gallatin"; the family moved to Sumner County in November 1815, and David "was for some years engaged in banking" there. It was in Tennessee that the family became intimate with General Andrew Jackson.[2]

In 1820 David and his brother-in-law Perry Nowland moved to Florence, Alabama.[2] Hallum says that David was editing the *Florence Gazette* "in the interest of democracy" at the time of a letter from Jackson dated April 1823.[2] Letters from his sons William and John, written from Little Rock in May and June 1829, are addressed to "David Fulton, Esq., Florence, Alabama".[2]

His wife died in Lauderdale County, Alabama, in the 1820s; the sources disagree about the year (see Open questions).[3][8][10]

## Little Rock

William S. Fulton went to Little Rock in May 1829 as Secretary of Arkansas Territory.[2] A Little Rock local-history article says that David followed in 1833, that his daughter Jane Juliet Shall and her children came as well, and that the Fultons and Shalls rented the Hinderliter House in 1834.[3] That building is now part of Historic Arkansas Museum, which the same article says was founded by one of his descendants, Louise Loughborough.[3]

The City of Little Rock's list of mayors shows David Fulton as the fourth mayor of the Town of Little Rock, from January to November 1835. His term ended early because a new charter turned the town into a city and a new election was held in November 1835.[5] His son was appointed governor of the Territory on 9 March 1835, so that for some months the father was mayor of the capital while the son was governor.[2][3]

The same local-history article says that he was proprietor of the Tan Yard, a tanning business in Little Rock; president of the Anti-Gambling Society; a justice of the peace of Pulaski County; and county judge of Pulaski County from 1836 to 1838.[3] No contemporary record of these has been seen for this biography.

## Surveyor-general

Hallum says that David Fulton "was surveyor-general of Arkansas by appointment from President Van Buren".[2] The local-history article dates the appointment to 1838.[3] In his letter of 4 January 1841, written after the Whig victory of 1840, David told his son that it was generally expected that "all the Federal officers of any note will certainly be removed on the coming in of the new administration", and went on:

> And were it not for the sake of those depending on me for support, I would cheerfully at this moment resign the office of surveyor-general and the responsibility it imposes, but on account of those who look to me, I am somewhat desirous to hold the present office, until the term of appointment expires, which will be two years from the eighth of this month. And it is my first term, so that rotation in office cannot operate against me; nor was any one turned out to make room for me.[2]

Whether he was removed, or served out the term to January 1843, has not been found.

## Death

David Fulton died at Little Rock on 7 August 1843, aged seventy-two, and was buried in Mount Holly Cemetery, where several others of the family lie.[3][4] His son William died a year later, on 15 August 1844.[2]

## Family

No single source lists all his children. Those named in the sources read are:

* **William Savin Fulton** (1795–1844), who married Matilda Nowland.[2][7]
* **Mary**, "the eldest daughter", who married William Crockett of Gallatin, Tennessee, in 1815.[2]
* **Eliza**, who married Dr Elmore Douglass at Gallatin.[2] Find a Grave links to David a daughter Elizabeth Savin "Eliza" Fulton Douglass (1797–1835).[8]
* **Jane Juliet**, who married first a Mr Shall and second Dr Holt, an army surgeon, and lived in Little Rock.[3][7] Find a Grave gives her dates as 1801–1891.[8]
* **Rebecca**, "the third daughter", who married Alfred Douglass, a cousin of Dr Elmore Douglass.[2] Find a Grave gives Rebecca Louise Fulton Douglass (1806–1882).[8]
* **Dr John T. Fulton**, who was in Little Rock by June 1829, when he wrote to his father that the place offered little to "a medical man".[2] Fay Hempstead's history (1890) lists him in 1830 as one of the town's five doctors and keeper of its one drugstore, and as postmaster until 1832.[9] The parish history says that he married Caroline Scull and "removed from Little Rock at an early date".[7]

## Residences

* **County Donegal, Ireland**, until 1788.[2][4]
* **Maryland**, from September 1788: Cecil County, where his son was born in 1795, and Baltimore.[2][6][7]
* **Gallatin, Sumner County, Tennessee**, from November 1815 to 1820.[2]
* **Florence, Lauderdale County, Alabama**, from 1820; still there in June 1829.[2]
* **Little Rock, Arkansas**, from 1833 according to a modern account; the Hinderliter House in 1834.[3]

## Open questions

* **His relationship to Theo Armour.** The line runs David Fulton, William S. Fulton, Sophia Fulton (Mrs George C. Watkins), Ida Watkins (Mrs Marshall Shirk), Anne Bell Shirk (Mrs Burrage), Cynthia Burrage, Theo Armour.[7][10][12] The Shirk page in this repository notes that "by another account" Ida Watkins was a granddaughter, not a daughter, of Chief Justice Watkins; if that were so the count of generations would change.[12]
* **Date of his marriage.** The local-history article says June 1795.[3] Find a Grave's family links say 1794.[8] The profile page says 1795.[1] The eldest known child was born on 2 June 1795, which sits badly with a marriage in June 1795. No marriage record has been seen.
* **His wife's death.** The local-history article says November 1829; Find a Grave says 24 November 1829; the one family tree in this repository that records her says November 1824, and cites the same Find a Grave memorial.[3][8][10] Find a Grave and the tree give her birth as 20 December 1764 in Baltimore County, Maryland, which would make her six years older than her husband.[8][10] Her burial place is not known.[8]
* **His birthplace and parents.** County Donegal is given by Find a Grave and the family tree; the parish of Templemore and the parents' names rest on a single modern article, which gives no source.[4][10] The parish history of 1900 says only "of Baltimore, Md."[7]
* **When he came to Little Rock.** The local-history article says 1833.[3] The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says that after 1829 William was able to find places for relatives and that "his father became postmaster of Little Rock".[6] Hempstead names Dr John T. Fulton, the son, as the postmaster from 1829 to 1832.[9] The Encyclopedia may have confused father and son, or David may have come earlier than 1833; this has not been settled.
* **The Shall children.** The local-history article says Jane Juliet Shall came with four children; the parish history of 1900 names three.[3][7]
* **Date of the surveyor-general appointment.** The local-history article says 1838.[3] David's own letter says his term would end on 8 January 1843, which points to a commission dated 8 January of an earlier year; the commission itself has not been seen.[2]
* **Who edited the *Florence Gazette*.** Hallum says David was editing it in 1823.[2] William's diary, printed in the same book, says "I wrote all the editorials for the Florence Gazette, since October, 1821".[2]
* **"Judge".** Hallum and the parish history call him Judge David Fulton.[2][7] The only judgeship named in the sources read is the Pulaski County one of 1836–1838.[3]
* **The profile page.** Its text appears to come from an AI chat linked at its head, and it cites no sources.[1] It omits the years in Tennessee, and it says that Mount Holly Cemetery is "often called the 'Westminster Abbey of Arkansas'", which has not been checked.

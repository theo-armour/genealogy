# Francis Hathaway Haskell

**Francis Hathaway Haskell** (about 1827 or 1830 – 1868), known as Frank, was a Massachusetts shipmaster who settled in south China. In 1856 he was the first master of the Boston ship *Norseman*, in which he sailed to San Francisco and China. By 1861 he was a partner in Thomas Hunt & Co., shipwrights at Whampoa, and from about 1864 he commanded the river steamer *Kinshan* between Hong Kong and Canton.[1][2][3][4] He married Elizabeth Russell, and their children were born in China. The family trees say he died on 25 June 1868; no contemporary record of his death has been found (see Open questions).[5][6] He was the father of [Alice Hathaway Haskell Burrage](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Alice-Hathaway-Haskell-Burrage-1861-1947/README-Alice-Hathaway-Haskell-Burrage-1861-1947.md) and Theo Armour's maternal great-great-grandfather.[7]

## Family and early life

Francis Hathaway Haskell was a son of Elisha Haskell, a Boston auctioneer, and his second wife, Alice Russell Hathaway of New Bedford.[5][8] The *Chronicles of the Haskell Family* (1943) lists him among their six children as "Francis H. m Elizabeth Russell".[8] His father was in Boston by 1826 and lived at 2 Tremont Place from 1830 to at least 1845, so he probably grew up there; see his father's biography.[9]

Geni gives his birth as 1830, at "Boston or New Bedford"; two Ancestry-derived trees give 1827 at Boston.[5][6] No birth record has been seen. Geni records his nickname as "Frank", and a book of 1918 on old Boston sea captains calls him "Frank Haskell".[3][5]

His sister Caroline Watts Haskell married George Cabot Lee at Boston on 10 December 1857. Their daughter Alice Hathaway Lee, born in 1861, was the first wife of Theodore Roosevelt.[10]

The profile page says that the 1850 census lists him as a seaman in his father's household in Boston.[1] The census has not been seen for this biography.

## Master of the ship *Norseman*

The ship *Norseman*, of Boston, was built at Boston in 1856 and registered there on 17 May 1856. She measured a little under 812 tons and was 163 feet 5 inches long, with two decks and three masts. The register names her master as Francis H. Haskell. Four fifths of her belonged to the copartners Arthur Cunningham, John A. Cunningham and C. Loring Cunningham, and one fifth to "Elisha H. Haskell", of Boston, who was probably his father.[2] According to Geni, his sister Alice had married John Adams Cunningham Jr in 1850.[5]

The *New York Times* in the spring of 1856 reported the "Ship Norseman, (of Boston,) Haskell" arriving from Boston in tow of a steam tug.[11] Carl Cutler's history of the clipper ships lists a passage of the *Norseman*, Haskell, from New York, 10 June, arriving 31 October, in 144 days; this was apparently her first passage to San Francisco, in 1856.[12] A San Francisco shipping list of November 1856 has the entry "Am ship Norseman, Haskell, Manila".[13]

She came home from China early in 1858. The *New York Times* of 29 March 1858 reported:

> Ship Norseman, (of Boston,) Haskell, Macao via Hong Kong Nov. 26, St. Helena Feb. 16, passed Anjier Dec. 14 with teas and silks to order.[14]

The same issue names her passengers from Macao as "Mrs. Henry Carrol and child" and "Mrs. F. H. Haskell".[14] He was therefore married by November 1857.

In 1918 the State Street Trust Company printed a list, drawn up by Captain Arthur H. Clark, of Boston captains and their family connections. It includes:

> Frank Haskell, who commanded the "Norseman," was an elder brother of Thomas and Frederick Haskell.[3]

## Thomas Hunt & Co., Whampoa

Within a few years he had left the sea for business in China. Edward Kenneth Haviland's study of American steam navigation in China says that F. H. Haskell was admitted a partner in Thomas Hunt and Co. on 1 July 1861, and that he was also associated with Captain James Bridges Endicott, an American shipowner in China who was a director of the Hong Kong, Canton and Macao Steamboat Company.[4] Thomas Hunt and Co. had a shipbuilding establishment at Whampoa, the anchorage below Canton.[4] The *Chronicle and Directory for China* for 1864 lists "Haskell, F., partner T. Hunt & Co shipwrights, Whampoa".[15]

Haviland names him as the registered owner of the steamer *Kumsing*, of 467 tons, built at Whampoa in 1862, where the name is also spelled "Haskall".[4] He also had a part in bringing out the river steamer *Kinshan*, a wooden paddle steamer that was fabricated by Roosevelt and Joyce and sent out to China in pieces, to be put together there:

> Upon the steamer's arrival in China, F. H. Haskell, acting for himself, James B. Endicott, Thomas Hunt and Co. and others, agreed to transfer her to A. F. Heard. It was estimated that the cost of the steamboat, completed in China, would be $133,900, of which Heard was to pay $70,300 and Haskell undertook to raise the remaining $60,000.[4]

*Kinshan* was set up at Thomas Hunt and Co.'s yard at Whampoa. Her keel was laid on 12 March 1863, she was launched on 3 May, and she ran her trials at Hong Kong on 30 October.[4]

In April, May and June 1864 the *Hong Kong Daily Press* carried a notice from Thomas Hunt & Co., dated Hongkong, 31 March, that "the Interest and Responsibility of Mr. F. H. Haskell in our Firm ceased on this day".[16]

Haviland adds that "it seems that Mrs. Endicott and Mrs. Haskell were sisters".[4] See the biography of [Elizabeth Russell](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Elizabeth-Russell-1835-1909/README-Elizabeth-Russell-1835-1909.md).

## Commander of the *Kinshan*

The *Chronicle and Directory* for 1865 lists "Haskell, F., commander river str. Kinshan, Augustine Heard & Co."[15] Advertisements in the *Hong Kong Daily Press* in 1865 and 1866 announce that "the Steamer 'Kin Shan,' Captain Haskell, will leave Hongkong for Canton", and by October 1866 she was advertised by the Hongkong, Canton and Macao Steam-boat Company, Limited, as "'Kin-Shan,' Capt. Haskell".[17] The shipping lists of the *China Mail* name Haskell as her commander from 1866 until the end of June 1868.[18]

None of these lists gives the captain's initials. That he was Francis H. Haskell rests on Haviland's account, on the directory entries for "Haskell, F." and on the birth notice of 1867 quoted below, which calls him "Capt. F. H. Haskell" at Hong Kong.[4][15][19]

In September 1865 the *London and China Telegraph* printed an account of the burning of the steamer *Hankow*, in which a Captain Haskell and his officers and engineers came on board to help her captain fight the fire. The name of Haskell's own steamer is garbled in the scan and is probably *Kinshan*.[20]

## Marriage and children

He married Elizabeth Russell.[8][21] No record of the marriage has been found. She was travelling as "Mrs. F. H. Haskell" in November 1857, and their daughter Bessie was born about the autumn of 1859.[14][22] Geni's date for the marriage, "about 1860", is therefore too late.[5]

The children known from the sources read are:

* **Elizabeth, called Bessie.** The *London and China Telegraph* of 10 June 1865 printed her death "at Canton, very suddenly, of cholera", aged five years and six months, as the daughter of Francis H. and Elizabeth R. Haskell. The scan gives the date as "April 31st" and the father's name is partly illegible.[22] Geni lists an Elizabeth Hathaway Haskell, without dates, who is probably the same child.[5]
* **[Alice Hathaway Haskell](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Alice-Hathaway-Haskell-Burrage-1861-1947/README-Alice-Hathaway-Haskell-Burrage-1861-1947.md)**, born on 6 October in 1857, 1861 or 1862, in Hong Kong or "China"; she married Albert Cameron Burrage in 1885 and died in 1947. Her husband's Harvard class report names her parents as "Francis Hathaway Haskell and Elizabeth (Russell) Haskell".[7][21]
* **Francis Hathaway Haskell Jr**, born at Hong Kong on 19 July 1863 and died at Brookline, Massachusetts, on 1 March 1927, according to Geni.[5]
* **Robert Hathaway Haskell**, born at Hong Kong and died in China, according to Geni, which gives no dates.[5]
* **Sarah Anne Haskell**, born at Hong Kong, according to Geni.[5] The *London and China Telegraph* of 28 August 1867 printed the birth "on the 9th July, at Hong Kong, the wife of Capt. F. H. Haskell, of a daughter".[19] The notice does not name the child; Sarah is the likeliest.

## Death

His profile page and two Ancestry-derived family trees give the date of his death as 25 June 1868. The two trees give the place as "Lost overboard en route from Hong Kong to New York".[1][6] None of them cites a source. Geni gives no date or place of death, and carries the note "Master of ship Norsemen".[5]

Nothing found for this biography confirms or contradicts the date. The shipping lists of the *China Mail* still name Haskell as commander of the *Kin Shan* in the issues of 25, 26, 27 and 30 June 1868, but such lists were standing matter and may not have been corrected at once.[18] No death notice was found in searches of the scanned *China Mail* and *Hong Kong Daily Press* for 25 June to 3 July 1868, or of the *London and China Telegraph* for July to December 1868. The scans are poor, so this proves little.

His widow was later of Roxbury and Boston, Massachusetts; she died in 1909.[7][23]

## Residences

* **Boston, Massachusetts**, where his father lived from 1826.[9]
* **At sea**, as master of the ship *Norseman*, from 1856 to at least 1858.[2][14]
* **Whampoa, Canton and Hong Kong**, from about 1861 until 1868. A daughter died at Canton in 1865 and another was born at Hong Kong in 1867.[4][15][19][22]

## Open questions

* **Year and place of birth.** Geni and the profile page give 1830, "Boston or New Bedford".[1][5] Two family trees give 1827 at Boston.[6] His parents' intention of marriage was published on 1 July 1826, and Geni gives his elder sister Alice's birth as 1829.[5][9] The folder name uses 1830.
* **Date, place and manner of death.** 25 June 1868, "lost overboard en route from Hong Kong to New York", rests on unsourced family trees.[6] His daughter Alice's profile page, as reported in her biography, says only that he died "sometime before 1899".[7] No newspaper report, consular record or probate has been found.
* **Whether he was still master of the *Norseman* after 1858.** Geni's note calls him master of the ship "Norsemen" without dates.[5] The sources read place him ashore in China by 1861.[4]
* **The profile page's description of him.** The profile page says he was "likely" a "merchant or ship captain involved in the China Trade" and gives one child. *(Unsound: from an AI-generated summary, with no source.)*[1] The sources above show that he was a shipmaster, then a partner in a Whampoa shipyard, then a river-steamer captain, with at least five children.
* **Date of the withdrawal from Thomas Hunt & Co.** The notice is dated 31 March; the year reads 1863 in some scans and 1864 in others. It ran in the newspaper from April 1864, and the directory for 1864 still lists him as a partner, so 1864 is more likely.[15][16]
* **His daughter Alice's year of birth.** Her biography sets out the conflict between 1857, 1861 and 1862.[7] Two points found here bear on it. Mrs F. H. Haskell sailed from Macao in the *Norseman* in November 1857 with no child named beside her, although another passenger's child was listed.[14] And Bessie, born about the autumn of 1859, and Francis Jr, born in July 1863, leave room for a birth in October 1861 more easily than in October 1862.[5][22] Neither point is conclusive.
* **Bessie and "Elizabeth Hathaway Haskell".** Geni's undated Elizabeth Hathaway Haskell is assumed here to be the Bessie who died in 1865.[5][22]
* **"Elisha H. Haskell".** The part-owner of the *Norseman* is given a middle initial that his father has in no other record read.[2]
* **Other Captain Haskells.** There were several shipmasters of the name. The Boston registers also list a Francis Haskell and a Francis Haskell 2d, of Deer Isle, as masters of smaller vessels, and a Captain Haskell who died of apoplexy on board his ship in China in September 1853 is a different man.[2][24]

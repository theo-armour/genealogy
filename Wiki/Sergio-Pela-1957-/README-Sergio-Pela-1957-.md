# Sergio Pelà

**Sergio Pelà** (born 1957) is an Italian member of the Kudashev family. He is the son of Achille Pelà (1921–1986) and of Olga Sergeyevna Kudasheva (born 1926), the daughter of Prince [Sergei Vladimirovich Kudashev](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933/README-Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933.md) and his second wife, [Olga Erikovna von Pistohlkors](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Olga-Ericovna-von-Pistohlkors-1888-1963/README-Olga-Ericovna-von-Pistohlkors-1888-1963.md).[1][2][3] His mother was the half-sister of Princess [Maria (Myra) Sergeyevna Kudasheva](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Maria-Sergeyevna-Kudasheva-1896-1990/README-Maria-Sergeyevna-Kudasheva-1896-1990.md), my grandmother, so Sergio is my father's half-first cousin. He taught high school in Japan.[1] He keeps family papers and photographs, including views of his grandfather's house in Florence, Villa Granduchessa, which have helped the research into the Koudacheff painting collection.[4]

## Family

Sergio's grandfather, Prince Sergei Vladimirovich Kudashev, was a chamberlain at the court of Nicholas II and a collector of Dutch and Flemish paintings. He escaped Petrograd with his family in 1917–1918 and settled in Florence after his first wife, Princess Vera Maximilianovna, died in Paris in January 1920.[3] In Florence on 10 April 1922 he married Olga Erikovna von Pistohlkors, a daughter of Princess Paley by her first marriage.[2]

Their only child, Olga Sergeyevna Kudasheva, was born in 1926, the same year her father obtained Italian recognition of his princely title.[2][3] She was six or seven when her father died in Florence in February 1933.[2] In 1955 she married Achille Pelà.[2]

Achille Pelà was born on 7 November 1921 and died in 1986.[1] My notes record him, with a Rosanna Pelà, as a translator of Eddy Bauer's *Histoire controversée de la deuxième guerre mondiale* for the Istituto Geografico De Agostini, published in Italian in 1976.[1] Another note calls him an Italian historian and translator.[5] Neither has been checked against the book itself. How Rosanna Pelà was related to him is not recorded.

Achille and Olga had three children, according to my notes:[1]

* Alexandra Pelà, born about 1956
* Sergio Pelà, born 14 January 1957 (another note gives 1956)
* Olga Pelà, born about 1964

## Family papers

Sergio holds papers and photographs that came down from his grandfather.[4] The [Koudacheff collection catalogue](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Sergei-Vladimirovich-Kudashev-1863-1933/Koudacheff-Collection/koudacheff-collection.md) on this site cites them in two places:

* **A painting.** *The satyr and the nymph* by Victor Wolfvoet (entry 90, panel, 40 × 48 cm). It was in the 1902 St. Petersburg catalogue of the collection and in the Amsterdam auction of 27 June 1905 (lot 32). The catalogue gives its present provenance as the Sergio Pelà collection, and cites his family papers and correspondence with him.[4]
* **Two photographs of Villa Granduchessa.** Interior views of Prince Sergei's house in Florence, from the Sergio Pelà family archive. Five paintings that can be seen in them are listed in the catalogue as "under investigation": two portraits of an unknown gentleman, a portrait of a young man, a portrait of an unknown lady, and a still life.[4]

This folder also holds three photographs, taken on 6 September 2026, of the patent of King Victor Emmanuel III recognising Sergei Kudashev's princely title. The patent follows the Royal Decree of 3 September 1926 and is dated at Rome on 6 February 1927, with the signatures of the King and of Mussolini ([adocument.zip](adocument.zip)).

## Oask?! and the Movement of '77

A Sergio Pelà was one of the editors of *Oask?!*, the hand-written magazine of the Indiani Metropolitani (Metropolitan Indians), the ironic, anti-political wing of the Italian student movement of 1977. According to the Beinecke Library's guide to its collection of the movement's underground press, *Oask?!* was founded in Rome in March 1977. Its editors included Maurizio Gabbianelli, Massimo Terracini, Pablo Echaurren, Roberto Di Reda, Francesco Saglio, Sergio Pelà and Gloria Malatesta.[6]

Whether that Sergio Pelà is my cousin has not been confirmed. He would have been twenty in 1977. My notes also say he took part in the movement in Milan and wrote for the Milan youth circles (*Circoli del proletariato giovanile*). That came from an AI search summary, and the sources it cited could not be read. The one source that could be read places *Oask?!* in Rome, not Milan.[1][6]

## Discrepancies and open questions

* **Year of birth.** My family list gives 14 January 1957. Another note gives 1956, and his sister Alexandra is listed as born about 1956.[1] This folder uses 1957.
* **The editor of *Oask?!*.** Is this the same Sergio Pelà? A question for Sergio.
* **Milan or Rome.** The AI summary in my notes puts the movement in Milan. The Beinecke page says *Oask?!* was founded in Rome.[1][6]
* **His mother as a translator.** The same AI summary says Olga Koudacheff, like Achille and Rosanna Pelà, worked as a translator for De Agostini. No source has been seen for this.[1]

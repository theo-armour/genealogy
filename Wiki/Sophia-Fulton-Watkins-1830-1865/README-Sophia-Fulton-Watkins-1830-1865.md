# Sophia Fulton Watkins (1830–1865)

No portrait of her is held in this folder.

**Sophia Fulton Watkins** (1830? – 1865?) was a daughter of [William Savin Fulton](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/William-S-Fulton-1795-1844/README-William-S-Fulton-1795-1844.md), the last governor of Arkansas Territory and one of the state's first United States senators. She was one of only three of his eight children to live to adulthood. She married first James M. Curran, a lawyer, and second [George C. Watkins](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/George-Claiborne-Watkins-1815-1872/README-George-Claiborne-Watkins-1815-1872.md), Chief Justice of Arkansas. According to the 1900 history of Christ Church parish, Little Rock, she was the mother of [Ida Watkins Shirk](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Ida-Watkins-Shirk-1864-1951/README-Ida-Watkins-Shirk-1864-1951.md) and so Theo Armour's great-great-grandmother.[1][2] Her dates come from a single family tree.

## Life

She was a daughter of William Savin Fulton and Matilda Frances Nowland. The Encyclopedia of Arkansas names eight children and says that only Elizabeth, Sophia and Ida lived to be adults.[1] One family tree gives her birth in the District of Columbia on 4 April 1830, and records her there in 1842. Her father was then in Washington as a senator.[1][3]

She married first James M. Curran, a lawyer. One tree gives the marriage in Pulaski County, Arkansas, on 21 June 1849.[1][3] She married second George C. Watkins, later Chief Justice of Arkansas, as his second wife. The parish history of 1900 lists the children of the second marriage as Maria Louise, Elizabeth Wright (died), Georgie Claiborne and Ida Watkins.[1][2]

One tree cites the 1860 census for her at Little Rock, and gives her death there on 23 July 1865 and her burial at Little Rock.[3] If those dates are right, her daughter Ida was about seventeen months old.

## Open questions

* **Was she Ida's mother?** The 1900 parish history says so. A 1915 newspaper excerpt makes Ida a granddaughter of Chief Justice Watkins instead.[2]
* **Her dates.** Only one tree gives them, and it cites no record for her birth or death.[3]
* **Children of the first marriage.** The same tree lists an Alice Jimora Conway and a Mary C. Watkins under her marriage to Curran. Neither is confirmed.[3]

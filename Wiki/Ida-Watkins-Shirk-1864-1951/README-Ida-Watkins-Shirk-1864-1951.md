# Ida Watkins Shirk (1864–1951)

No portrait of her is held in this folder.

**Ida Watkins Shirk** (13 February 1864 – 21 November 1951) was born in Little Rock, Arkansas, a daughter of [Chief Justice George C. Watkins](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/George-Claiborne-Watkins-1815-1872/README-George-Claiborne-Watkins-1815-1872.md) and [Sophia Fulton](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Sophia-Fulton-Watkins-1830-1865/README-Sophia-Fulton-Watkins-1830-1865.md). She married the Philadelphia architect [J. C. Marshall Shirk](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/James-Cameron-Marshall-Shirk-1865-1918/README-James-Cameron-Marshall-Shirk-1865-1918.md) and was widowed in 1918. She died at Ipswich, Massachusetts. She was the mother of [Anne Bell Shirk Burrage](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Anne-Bell-Burrage-1890-1983/README-Anne-Bell-Burrage-1890-1983.md) and Theo Armour's great-grandmother, his mother's mother's mother.[1][2][3]

## Life

She was born at Little Rock on 13 February 1864, according to Geni and one family tree.[3][4] A history of Christ Church parish in Little Rock, published in 1900, lists her among the children of Chief Justice George C. Watkins and his second wife, Sophia Fulton, who was a daughter of Governor William S. Fulton of Arkansas.[1] If the tree's dates are right, her mother died when Ida was a year old and her father when she was eight.[4] One tree cites the 1870 census for her at Little Rock.[4]

She married James C. Marshall Shirk, a Philadelphia architect. One tree gives the marriage at Philadelphia on 20 April 1892.[2] The parish history records: "Ida married Marshall Shirk; they have one daughter, Anne Bell Shirk."[1] When Anne Bell's wedding to Albert Cameron Burrage Jr was announced in 1915, her address was given as 1339 Pine Street, Philadelphia.[1]

Her husband died in August 1918, and she lived another 33 years as a widow.[2] The 1950 census, cited by one tree, records her at Ipswich, Massachusetts, as a widowed mother-in-law in the household.[4] She died at Ipswich on or about 21 November 1951.[3][4]

## Open questions

* **Daughter or granddaughter of Chief Justice Watkins?** The 1900 parish history makes her his daughter. A Little Rock newspaper excerpt of 1915, seen only as search-engine OCR text, calls her his granddaughter. The parish history is the more detailed source.[1] If she was a granddaughter, her parents are not the couple named here.
* **Date of marriage.** 1892 comes from one tree. Anne Bell's year of birth is given as both 1890 and 1893. Her biography discusses this.[1][2]

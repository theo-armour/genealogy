# George Claiborne Watkins (1815–1872)

**George Claiborne Watkins** (1815 – 1872) was a Kentucky-born lawyer of Little Rock and Chief Justice of Arkansas. His second wife was [Sophia Fulton](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Sophia-Fulton-Watkins-1830-1865/README-Sophia-Fulton-Watkins-1830-1865.md), daughter of Governor [William S. Fulton](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/William-S-Fulton-1795-1844/README-William-S-Fulton-1795-1844.md). According to the 1900 history of Christ Church parish, Little Rock, their daughter was [Ida Watkins Shirk](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Ida-Watkins-Shirk-1864-1951/README-Ida-Watkins-Shirk-1864-1951.md), Theo Armour's great-grandmother. That would make him Theo's great-great-grandfather. One later account makes Ida his granddaughter instead (see Open questions).[1][2]

## Life

One family tree gives his birth at Shelbyville, Kentucky, on 25 November 1815, as a son of Isaac Watkins and Maria Toncray. Geni gives only the year and Kentucky.[3][4]

He became a lawyer at Little Rock. One tree cites the 1860 and 1870 censuses for him there, described as "Attorney At Law" and "Lawyer".[4] He is called Chief Justice of Arkansas in the 1900 parish history and in a Little Rock newspaper of 1915.[1]

He married, as his second wife, Sophia Fulton, the widow of the lawyer James M. Curran. The parish history lists the children of this marriage as Maria Louise, Elizabeth Wright (died), Georgie Claiborne and Ida, and adds: "Ida married Marshall Shirk; they have one daughter, Anne Bell Shirk."[1][2] Sophia died in July 1865, according to one tree.[4]

He died in 1872, at Little Rock according to Geni. One tree gives 7 December 1872 and his burial at Little Rock.[3][4]

## Open questions

* **Ida Watkins: daughter or granddaughter?** The 1900 parish history makes Ida his daughter by Sophia Fulton. A 1915 newspaper excerpt calls her mother (Ida) a granddaughter of "Chief Justice George Claiborne Watkins".[1] This question decides whether he is Theo's great-great-grandfather or one generation further back.
* **His first marriage.** Who was his first wife? The children Georgie, Claibourne and Walter in one tree may belong to it.[4]
* **His career.** When was he Chief Justice, and what other offices did he hold? None of the sources read for this page gives dates.
* **The Waltons.** The Burrage read-me links a Geni profile for his father, Isaac Watkins, and asks whether the family is related to "**the** Waltons".[5]

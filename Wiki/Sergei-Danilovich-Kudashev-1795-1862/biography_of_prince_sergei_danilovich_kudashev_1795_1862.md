# Prince Sergei Danilovich Kudashev

**Prince Sergei Danilovich Kudashev** (Russian: Князь Сергей Данилович Кудашев; 1795 – 1862) was a Russian nobleman, Imperial courtier, and civil administrator who served as Vice-Governor of Kiev Governorate in 1844 and held the court rank of *Kammerherr* (Chamberlain) and the civil rank of Active State Councillor.[1][2] A prominent landowner in Chigirin Uyezd of Kiev Governorate, he was the younger brother of Napoleonic war hero Major General Prince Nikolai Danilovich Kudashev, the father-in-law of the Berdyaev family (making him the maternal grandfather of Russian religious philosopher Nikolai Berdyaev), and the grandfather of St. Petersburg art collector Prince Sergei Vladimirovich Kudashev.[2][3][4]

## Family background and early life

Sergei Danilovich Kudashev was born in 1795 into the Kudashev family, an ancient Russian princely dynasty of Tatar origin whose princely title had been confirmed within the Russian nobility since the 17th century.[2][5] His parents were Prince Daniil Ivanovich Kudashev (1760–1845) and Yekaterina Sergeyevna Baranova.[2]

In 1804, his father purchased the estate of Veselyi Kut (*Весёлый Кут*) in Chigirin Uyezd of Kiev Governorate (modern-day Cherkasy Oblast, Ukraine), establishing the family's southern landed seat in Right-Bank Ukraine.[6] Sergei's elder brother, Major General Prince Nikolai Danilovich Kudashev (1784–1813), married Yekaterina Mikhailovna Kutuzova—daughter of Field Marshal Prince Mikhail Kutuzov—and achieved distinction commanding a flying partisan detachment during the Patriotic War of 1812 before dying of wounds sustained at the Battle of Leipzig in October 1813.[2][5]

## Civil service and Vice-Governorship of Kiev

Entering Imperial civil and court service, Kudashev rose through the Table of Ranks to attain the fourth-class civil rank of Active State Councillor (*действительный статский советник*) and was appointed an Imperial *Kammerherr* (Chamberlain).[1][2]

In 1844, Kudashev served as **Vice-Governor of Kiev Governorate** (*Киевский вице-губернатор*), acting as senior deputy to the civil governor in administering one of the Russian Empire's most strategically and economically important southwestern provinces.[1] Residing in Kiev during his official tenure, he maintained close ties with the provincial nobility, the Kiev-Pechersk Lavra, and the intellectual and administrative circles of the city.[3][6]

## Kiev Governorate estates

Following the death of his father in 1845, Kudashev inherited and managed the family's principal estates in Chigirin Uyezd, centered on **Veselyi Kut** and the adjacent village settlement of **Kudashevo** (*Кудашево*).[6][7] In his 1864 topographical and historical survey of the province (*Сказания о населенных местностях Киевской губернии*), Kiev ecclesiastical archivist Lavrenty Pokhilevich recorded the transfer of Veselyi Kut to Prince Daniil Ivanovich Kudashev in 1804 and its subsequent stewardship under Prince Sergei Danilovich, documenting the agricultural holdings, rural population, and parish life of the Chigirin properties.[6]

## Marriage and descendants

Kudashev married **Countess Mathilde de Choiseul-Gouffier** (1806–1867), a member of the French aristocratic house of Choiseul-Gouffier that had settled in the Russian Empire following the French Revolution.[2][3] Her grandfather, Count Marie-Gabriel-Florent-Auguste de Choiseul-Gouffier (1752–1817), had served as French Ambassador to the Ottoman Empire before emigrating to Russia, where Emperor Paul I appointed him Director of the Imperial Academy of Arts and the Imperial Public Library.[2]

Through this marriage, the Kudashev household in Kiev bridged Russian provincial nobility and Western European aristocratic culture. The couple had several children, including:[2][3][8]

* **Prince Vladimir Sergeyevich Kudashev** (1833–1871), a Kiev Governorate landowner and collector of paintings, rare books, and coins; father of Imperial Chamberlain and art collector Prince Sergei Vladimirovich Kudashev (1863–1933).[2][9]
* **Prince Sergei Sergeyevich Kudashev** (1834–1915), a retired military colonel and Active State Councillor who served as Manager of the Moscow Branch of the State Noble Land Bank and published the multi-part historical work *История императора Александра Павловича* (*History of Emperor Alexander Pavlovich*, 1912).[2][8]
* **Princess Alexandra Sergeyevna Kudasheva** (1838–1912), who married cavalry officer and Kiev noble marshal Alexander Mikhailovich Berdyaev; their younger son was the noted Russian religious and political philosopher **Nikolai Alexandrovich Berdyaev** (1874–1948), who wrote in his autobiography *Self-Knowledge* (*Самопознание*) about his maternal Kudashev and Choiseul-Gouffier heritage.[3][4]
* **Princess Olga Sergeyevna Kudasheva**, who married into the southwestern provincial nobility.[2]

Prince Sergei Danilovich Kudashev died in 1862, aged 66 or 67, leaving his Chigirin estates to his sons.[2][6]

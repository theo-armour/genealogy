**George Allison Armour** (27 March 1856 – 8 June 1936) was a Chicago-born Princeton graduate of the class of 1877 who settled at Princeton, New Jersey, in 1895 and lived there for the rest of his life. A son of the Chicago grain-elevator proprietor George Armour, he was a hospital and library trustee and church builder in Chicago, and a collector of books, whose library was sold at auction in New York in 1937. His house at 83 Stockton Street, Princeton, was later given to Princeton University by his daughter and is now the residence of the university's president.[3][4][5][6][7][8] He was the father of the diplomat [Norman Armour Sr](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982/README-Norman-Armour-Sr-1887-1982.md) and Theo Armour's paternal great-grandfather.[1][14]

## Family and early life

George Allison Armour was born in Chicago on 27 March 1856. He gave that date and place himself, on oath, in a passport application of January 1920, and the family trees agree.[1][9][12][13] He was the second son of George Armour, a Scottish-born contractor and grain-elevator proprietor, and Barbara Allison.[1][9][12]

His elder brother John William died in 1865. His younger brothers were William (1858–1888) and [Allison Vincent Armour](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Allison-Vincent-Armour-1863-1941/README-Allison-Vincent-Armour-1863-1941.md) (1863–1941), the yachtsman and patron of scientific expeditions, and his sister was Mary Greenlee (1868–1958), who married the architect [Francis Meredyth Whitehouse](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Francis-Meredyth-Whitehouse-1848-1938/README-Francis-Meredyth-Whitehouse-1848-1938.md).[12][13]

Two family trees cite the 1860 census for the household at Waukegan, Lake County, Illinois, and the 1870 census in the First Ward of Chicago.[13]

## Princeton and Chicago

He belonged to the Princeton class of 1877. In the class's twenty-year record, *After Twenty Years* (1898), he answered the secretary's questions briefly: his residences since graduation were "Princeton and Chicago"; his degrees were "B. A. and M. A."; he had married at Chicago on 27 April 1882; and against one question he wrote simply "Election to membership in Princeton University".[3] In 1897 he was on the executive committee for the class's twentieth reunion, with M. Taylor Pyne and others.[3]

The roll of the Second Presbyterian Church of Chicago, his father's church, appears to show him received on profession of faith on 2 March 1877.[10] Two family trees cite the 1880 census for him in his father's house in Chicago, single, with the occupation "Lawyer".[13] His father died in June 1881.[10]

In the 1880s he appears among the trustees of St. Luke's Free Hospital, an Episcopal charity in Chicago.[11] John Crerar, his father's fellow trustee at the Second Presbyterian Church, named "George A. Armour" among the first directors of the library founded under his will, the John Crerar Library.[10]

He was also the builder of St. Clement's Church in Chicago. A. T. Andreas's *History of Chicago* (1886) says the church, at State and Twentieth streets, was opened for worship on St. Clement's day, 23 November 1884; the digitised text is damaged at this point but appears to say that it was erected at the cost of George A. Armour.[11] In 1887 Dean Charles R. Hale of Davenport, Iowa, arranged through a friend in St. Petersburg for the monastery at Chersonesus in the Crimea to send "the builder of St. Clements Ch. at Chicago" a stone from the ruins of the old church of St. Clement there; the surviving letters are addressed to "Mr. Armour", and the offer was of a marble block, part of a marble column with a cross cut in it, or a slab from the old iconostasis.[15]

## Marriage and children

On 27 April 1882, in Chicago, he married Harriette Cobb Foote.[3][4] She was born on 15 November 1859, the youngest child of Erastus Foote (1821–1893), a Bowdoin graduate and lawyer of Wiscasset, Maine, who moved his family to Chicago in 1868, and of Sarah Page Wood. She was a granddaughter of [Erastus Foote](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Erastus-Foote-1777-1856/README-Erastus-Foote-1777-1856.md) (1777–1856) of Wiscasset.[4] Her father and her husband's father were ordained elders of the Second Presbyterian Church of Chicago on the same occasion, in April 1877.[10]

Abram W. Foote's *Foote Family* (1907) lists seven children:[4]

* George, born in Chicago on 16 December 1884; died 30 May 1893. A note in the Geni tree says he died of diphtheria in Chicago at the age of eight.[12]
* Paul, born 4 July 1886; died 23 January 1887.
* Norman, born 14 October 1887, at Brighton, England, while his parents were there.[12][16] He became a career diplomat.
* Barbara, born 27 November 1889. She married the Rev. [Walter Lowrie](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Walter-Lowrie-1868-1959/README-Walter-Lowrie-1868-1959.md).[6]
* William, born 23 January 1892.
* Allison, born 27 August 1896; the family trees give the place as Manchester, Vermont.[13]
* Edmund, born 26 July, in a year printed as 1889 but evidently 1899. The family trees say he died at Concord, New Hampshire, on 4 June 1912.[12][13]

## Princeton

*Foote Family* gives the family's residence as Princeton, New Jersey, "since July, 1895".[4] In 1895 he acquired the Italianate house at what is now 83 Stockton Street, designed by the Philadelphia architect John Notman and built in 1845 by Commodore Robert F. Stockton for his son John P. Stockton; it had later been occupied by Paul Tulane.[6] Princeton University's own account says it "became the childhood home of Barbara Armour and her four brothers".[6] Princeton's records give "Allison House" as another name for the house, and a family tree records "Allison House, Princeton, N. J." as his address about 1918.[6][13]

The family trees cite the federal censuses of 1900, 1920 and 1930 and the New Jersey state censuses of 1905 and 1915 for him at Princeton; in 1915 his occupation is given as "Retired" and in 1920 as "None".[13] One tree cites a passenger list for his arrival at New York from Liverpool on 29 August 1914.[13]

On 12 January 1920 he applied for a passport for himself and his wife to travel to Italy and France, and to Belgium to "visit son", intending to sail from New York on the *Dante Alighieri* on 4 February.[9] His son Norman was then at the American Embassy in Brussels.[16]

Harriette Armour died on 11 October 1924, at Princeton according to the Geni tree.[12] One tree cites the 1930 census for him at Princeton as a widower.[13]

## Books

He was a collector of rare books. The Boston Public Library's copy of the 1614 edition of *Englands Helicon* carries "the bookplates of Frederick Spiegelberg and George Allison Armour" on its front pastedown.[5] After his death the American Art Association–Anderson Galleries in New York issued a catalogue, *The Library of the Late George Allison Armour, Princeton, N.J., to be Dispersed at Public Sale*, for a sale on 22 and 23 April 1937.[7] The catalogue itself has not been seen for this biography, so nothing is said here about the contents of the library.

## Death

He died at his home in Princeton on 8 June 1936, "in his eighty-first year". The funeral was at Trinity Church, Princeton, on Wednesday 10 June.[2] He was buried at Princeton.[12][13]

In 1930 his daughter Barbara and her husband Walter Lowrie had taken up residence in the Stockton Street house on their return from Rome. In 1960 she gave it to Princeton University in memory of her husband; it was a university guest house until 1968 and has since been the residence of the president of the university, under the name Walter Lowrie House.[6][8] The Wikipedia article on his father says that Armour Road, Allison Road and Campbelton Road in Princeton are named for George Armour, Barbara Allison Armour and Campbeltown.[17]

## Residences

* **Chicago, Illinois.** Born there in 1856; cited there in the censuses of 1870 and 1880; married there in 1882; his first child was born there in 1884.[4][9][13]
* **Waukegan, Lake County, Illinois.** The family is cited there in the 1860 census.[13]
* **Princeton, New Jersey**, as a student in the 1870s, and from July 1895 until his death in 1936: the John P. Stockton house, also called Allison House, now Walter Lowrie House, 83 Stockton Street.[3][4][6]
* **Brighton, England**, where his son Norman was born in October 1887.[12][16]
* **Manchester, Vermont**, where his son Allison is said to have been born in August 1896.[13]

## Open questions

* **Year of birth.** His own passport application and the family trees give 27 March 1856, and "in his eighty-first year" in June 1936 agrees.[2][9][12] The Boston Public Library's catalogue heading is "Armour, George Allison, 1855-1936", and the MyHeritage export has "about 1855".[5][12]
* **Occupation.** The 1880 census, as cited in the family trees, calls him a lawyer.[13] A Princeton University history page calls him "a wealthy Chicago businessman".[8] No source read says where he studied law, whether he practised, or what business he was in; the notes of one hired researcher call him "a yachtsman and businessman", which looks like confusion with his brother Allison.[13]
* **The list of children.** The profile page lists eight: "George, Paul, Norman, Barbara, William R., Allison Vincent, Edmond and Liam".[1] *Foote Family* (1907) lists seven and has no Liam, and Princeton's account speaks of Barbara "and her four brothers", that is, the four who outlived infancy and childhood.[4][6] "Liam" appears in the Geni-derived trees without any dates and is probably a duplicate of William.[13] The printed sources read give the names as William, Allison and Edmund, without "R." or "Vincent".[4]
* **His siblings.** The profile page lists "Mary Greenlee and Alice" as his sisters.[1] The Geni tree has an undated "Alice Whitehouse", wife of Frank Whitehouse, beside Mary Greenlee Armour, who married Francis M. Whitehouse; these are probably one person.[12]
* **Paul's death.** *Foote Family* and one tree give 23 January 1887; another tree gives 24 January 1887; Geni gives 1886.[4][12][13]
* **Barbara's birth and death years.** The folder name and profile page use 1889–1981, in agreement with *Foote Family* and two trees.[4][13] Geni has "about 27 November 1890" and a death in 1982.[12]
* **The younger Allison's dates.** Two trees give 27 August 1896 – 9 December 1955; Geni and MyHeritage give 1897–1945.[12][13]
* **Harriette's birthplace.** Geni gives Chicago.[12] Her husband's passport application says she was born at Wiscasset, Maine, which fits *Foote Family*: her family did not move to Chicago until 1868.[4][9]
* **Date of the marriage in one set of notes.** A hired researcher's notes give the marriage year as 1881; the class record and *Foote Family* both give 27 April 1882.[3][4][13]
* **When he came to Princeton.** *Foote Family* says July 1895, and Princeton says he acquired the house in 1895.[4][6] His son Allison's birth at Manchester, Vermont, in August 1896 suggests a summer residence there, but no source read says so.
* **St. Clement's Church.** Andreas gives its opening as November 1884, while the letters of 1887 speak of "the church now building in Chicago".[11][15] Andreas adds that the 1884 building was "only the precursor" of a more substantial one, which may explain this, but the printed page should be checked.
* **The house's date.** Princeton's text says the house was built in 1845; the heading of the same page says 1849.[6]

**Valerian Gavrilovich Karnovich** (Russian: Валериан Гаврилович Карнович; 1 April 1823 – 17 April 1891) was a Russian official and courtier. He rose from a cavalry cornet to the rank of Actual State Councillor in the Ministry of State Property, and held the court rank of chamberlain.[1][2] He was the father of [Princess Olga Paley](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Olga-Valerianovna-Paley-1865-1929/README-Olga-Valerianovna-Paley-1865-1929.md), and so a great-great-grandfather of [Sergio Pelà](https://theo-armour.github.io/genealogy/#Wiki/Sergio-Pela-1957-/README-Sergio-Pela-1957-.md).[1][2]

## Life

He was born in Moscow on 1 April 1823, the eldest son of Gavriil Stepanovich Karnovich. The parish register quoted on Geni records his birth, and his twin sister Maria's, in the house of his father's aunt, Nadezhda Ivanovna Kurmanaleyeva.[1]

His career is set out in the Malorossiysky rodoslovnik and in Yaroslavl archive papers, both quoted on Geni.[1] He entered the Orenburg Uhlan Regiment as a non-commissioned officer in July 1841, became a cornet, and retired as a lieutenant in April 1845. In January 1846 he became an assistant superintendent of Count Sheremetev's hospice in Moscow, and entered the civil ranks. In the 1850s he served in Yaroslavl, as an adviser to the provincial chamber of state property and from 1859 as chief superintendent of the Rybinsk provision stores. He was then attached to the Ministry of State Property, and became a collegiate councillor in 1864 and an Actual State Councillor in 1869. He was made a *kammerjunker* in 1863 and a chamberlain in 1867. In 1882 he sat on the council of the Minister of State Property and directed the St. Petersburg prison welfare committee.[1]

The Moscow confession register for 1848 lists him at 25, with his wife Olga Vasilyevna, 21, and two sons, Vladimir, 4, and Gavriil, 2.[1] In 1853 he held 188 serfs in Poshekhonye Uyezd and 240 in Vologda Uyezd. In 1882 he had 1,200 desyatinas of land in Yekaterinoslav Governorate and about 700 in Yaroslavl Governorate.[1] His daughter's page records that the family held the village of Pyatnitskaya Gora in Yaroslavl Governorate.[2]

Princess Paley's page gives his death on 17 April 1891.[2]

## Open questions

* **Where he died.** The Malorossiysky rodoslovnik says St. Petersburg. The *Provintsialny nekropol* lists him at Gora-Pyatnitskaya in Yaroslavl Uyezd, which is probably his burial place.[1]
* **The children.** A Yaroslavl archive note lists five children: Vladimir, Gavriil, Dionisy, Lyubov and Sergei. Geni adds a child who died in 1856, and Olga, born in 1865.[1]
* **Malorossiysky origins.** The Karnovich family is recorded in a genealogy of Little Russian (Ukrainian) families, and its nobility was proved in Chernigov. How it came to Moscow and Yaroslavl isn't recorded here.[1]
